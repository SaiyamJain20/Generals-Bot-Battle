## Recommendations for us (numbered, actionable)

Assumed knobs of tune/cma_tune.py (per coordinator): `--self-play`, `--league-every K --league-max M`, `--pfsp p` (opponent weight ~ (1-score)^p).
Throughput note [I]: runs/local1,local2 logs show ~240-385 s per generation (17 candidates incl. incumbent). One generation therefore ~ 1000 games, i.e. ~3 games/s; 1000 extra games for a final check cost ~5-6 min on the same workers. So final validation with 1-2k games is affordable.

1. Pool = anchors 55% / league 30% / self 15% (share of games, not of opponents). [I, grounded in OpenAI Five 80/20 self/past and AlphaStar PFSP]
   - Fixed anchors (never change, 55%): ResBot-BC 30%, Hunter 10%, hunter_castles/rusher/turtle 15% combined. These stop cycling and keep the signal "beats an external strong-ish style".
   - League (30%): past snapshots of OUR bot. `--league-every 8 --league-max 6` (a snapshot of es.mean every 8 gens ~ every 45 min; keep the 6 newest plus never drop the very first tuned one). [I; OpenAI Five adds a past self every 10 iterations [V]]
   - Self (15%): current es.mean via `--self-play`. Do not exceed ~20%: current-vs-current gives ~0.5 for everyone, almost no gradient, and invites cycles [V: Lux S2 write-up quote below; quant-eagle README says mirror self-play can cycle].
2. `--pfsp 1.0` (not 2) on the league + anchors, weights computed from the PREVIOUS generation's incumbent/mean scores, floor each weight at 0.05 before normalising, cap at 3x the mean weight. [I] AlphaStar's hard-opponent weighting is (1-x)^p; with only ~8-10 noisy opponents and 12 games each, p=2 puts most mass on one opponent whose score estimate is itself noise (+-0.14 per 12-game opponent estimate). Compute weights per generation from the incumbent, never from each candidate's own scores (otherwise weights are correlated with noise and fitness is biased).
3. Fitness = PFSP-weighted mean of per-opponent score. Do NOT use plain min. Log min and unweighted mean as diagnostics only. If you want robustness, use soft-min: fitness = 0.7*weighted_mean + 0.3*mean of the worst two opponents. [I] Min over noisy 12-game estimates selects for noise (order-statistic bias) and has near-zero gradient for the other 90% of the pool.
4. Games per candidate: keep 12 per opponent for ~6 opponents (72-80 games/candidate; SE of score ~0.056-0.058, ~40 Elo) with common random numbers (already done) and popsize 16-20 (keep 16 if CPU bound; 24 if you add the cluster). Do not spend more per candidate; spend extra on re-evaluation instead (rec 5). [I from UH-CMA-ES / re-evaluation literature]
5. Promotion rule (winner's-curse fix): the best-of-16 in a generation is biased upward (e.g. runs/local2 gen 2 best 0.879 vs same-params-style incumbent 0.686-0.83). Every 5 gens take the top-3 candidates plus es.mean, re-play each 100 games on FRESH map seeds against the full pool, promote only if it beats the incumbent by >= 0.03 (about 1 SE of a 100-game paired comparison ~0.05, so require 2 consecutive confirmations). Ship es.mean/the re-evaluated winner, not raw best-of-generation. [I; pycma docs note the mean is the better noisy estimate [U exact quote]]
6. Held-out opponents (never in the tuning pool): nanomena BC clone (data/bc/nanomena), ResBot-BC at BC_TEMP=0 (argmax; distribution shift vs the TEMP 0.3 anchor), one frozen early version (v0/t1) and expander. Every 10 gens evaluate es.mean on held-out with 100 games each. Alarm: pool score up >= 0.05 over 20 gens while held-out score flat or down >= 0.03. Then re-weight pool toward styles that the held-out set resembles, or widen sigma. [I]
7. Final selection: from the last 3 checkpoints (es.mean at gens N-20, N-10, N) pick by held-out + pool combined, then run a fixed-size match vs the previous shipped version: 600 games (300 side-swapped pairs, fresh maps). Accept if score >= 0.53 (95% CI lower bound > 0.5 needs ~0.54 at N=600: SE 0.0204 => +1.96 SE = 0.04) or use SPRT elo0=0, elo1=25, alpha=beta=0.05 (expected ~1000-1300 games when true gap is 25 Elo, ~330 when it is 50). [I derived, see Q5]
8. Time plan for ~20 h [I]: 0-1 h set up pool/holdout; 1-13 h main CMA run(s) (cluster 10 cores can run an independent seed with a different pool mix; compare on held-out; do not merge by averaging parameters); 13-17 h refine with sigma 0.08 and 2x games; 17-19 h final matches/SPRT; 1 h reserve. Restart CMA once (fresh covariance, x0 = best so far, sigma 0.1) if sigma < 0.05 before hour 10: premature convergence is common under noise.
9. Anti-RL robustness (details in Q8): (a) test and tune late game - RL self-play bots are trained mostly on games that end before turn ~600 [I], so deathtouch-era fortress/chase-kill and draw-avoidance are likely out of their distribution; (b) never rely on mountain-pocket-free assumptions: set up scenarios where the opponent must route around mountain dead ends and punish it; (c) exploit mode fixation: when the opponent is gathering/castling, time a deep strike; when it is attacking, counter-garrison; (d) keep play unpredictable within a game (deterministic argmax RL bots are "exploitable in principle" per quant-eagle README [V]); (e) zero forfeits remains the largest edge versus heavy-net entries that must fit 150 ms on one shared core.
10. Generic libraries: skip (Q7). Optional: compute a Nash/maximin mixture over the empirical payoff matrix (our candidates x pool) with a tiny LP offline to set pool weights (PSRO meta-solver idea), but PFSP is simpler and sufficient.

## Answers per question

### Q1/Q2: league / past versions / PFSP / self-play in the fitness
Evidence:
- AlphaStar: main agent trained with PFSP over a pool of mostly past versions, opponents sampled by win-rate weighting (harder = more often); main exploiters train only against the latest main agent and are then added to the pool; league exploiters play the whole league history. [V] https://github.com/nicholaschenai/ai-games/blob/main/papers/alphastar-final.md , https://arxiv.org/pdf/2104.06890 (mini-AlphaStar summary). The (1-x)^p weighting form with p=2 is my recollection of the paper [U].
- OpenAI Five: 80% of games vs latest parameters, 20% vs past versions; past opponents sampled by softmax of a quality score; new past agent added every 10 iterations; purpose is avoiding strategy collapse/forgetting. [V] https://arxiv.org/pdf/1912.06680
- Generals specific: the "zero" agent (arXiv 2507.06825) used opponent pool N=3 = three most recent versions; a new agent replaces the oldest once it reaches 45% win rate vs the pool. [V] https://arxiv.org/html/2507.06825v1 . AverageJoe (arXiv 2606.23348) is pure self-play from random init (no BC, no fixed opponents), curriculum on spawn distance (cap starts at 4 cells, raised gradually), 15.35M-param transformer, 4 days on 4xH200. [V] https://arxiv.org/html/2606.23348v1
- Self-play is a weak proxy for real strength: a Kaggle competitor reported small improvements cancel out in self-play (attacks are countered, mistakes shared by both copies go unexploited) [secondary summary via search; the Kaggle page itself did not load, U]. quant-eagle README: "mirror self-play can cycle", mitigated with a scripted expander in the league and a fixed-eval sidecar. [V] https://github.com/quant-eagle/generals-competition-rl-bot
- Coevolution literature: cyclic dynamics, evolutionary forgetting, disengagement; Hall-of-Fame (fitness against old individuals too) mitigates. [V via search summary] https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12374889/
Answer: yes, a small league of our own past versions helps vs a purely fixed pool, but only as a minority (30%) next to fixed external anchors; current-vs-current only 15% (it is a regulariser against overfitting to the anchors, not a source of gradient). Heuristic parameter populations rarely have strategy cycles as sharp as RL, but the parameters here (castle timing, garrison fractions) are exactly the kind that can rock-paper-scissors (rush vs turtle), so keep rusher/turtle/Hunter anchors permanently.
Exploiter idea (cheap): once per ~15 gens, run a short 1-hour CMA where the OPPONENT is our current mean and the optimised bot is a copy (an "exploiter"); if it reaches >0.65 vs the main bot, the main bot has a hole: add that exploiter snapshot to the league. [I]

### Q5: games needed / SPRT / Elo CIs
Derivations [I] (score S per game, draws ~1% so Bernoulli approx):
- dS/dElo = ln(10)/400 * S(1-S) = 0.00144 per Elo at S=0.5. SE of Elo from N games ~ 347/sqrt(N) (at S=0.5). N=100: 35; 400: 17; 600: 14; 1000: 11; 2000: 7.8. 95% CI = +-1.96 SE.
- Detect a gap D (95%, two-sided, fixed N): N ~ (1.96*347/D)^2 => D=100 Elo: 46 games; 50: 185; 30: 510; 20: 1155; 10: 4600.
- Convert: 0.53 score ~ +21 Elo; 0.55 ~ +35; 0.60 ~ +70; 0.65 ~ +108; 0.75 ~ +191.
- SPRT: LLR bounds for alpha=beta=0.05 are +-2.94 (matches dogeystamp: alpha .05 beta .1 gave 2.89 upper bound [V]). Expected games ~ 2.94 / KL per game, KL ~ delta^2/(2 S(1-S)) with delta = score gap. elo1=25 -> delta 0.036 -> KL 0.0026 -> ~1100 games; elo1=50 -> ~290; elo1=10 -> ~7000 (a real engine example: elo0=0, elo1=10, alpha .05 beta .1 stopped after 2026 games, measured +19.6 +- 13.2 Elo, because chess has many draws and the true gap was larger [V] https://www.dogeystamp.com/chess3/ ). Stockfish bounds used: gainer [0,2] STC / [0.5,2.5] LTC; weaker engines [0,10] [V] https://chessprogramming.org/Sequential_Probability_Ratio_Test ; Fishtest uses GSPRT (MLE for nuisance params) [V] https://official-stockfish.github.io/docs/fishtest-wiki/Fishtest-Mathematics.html
- Recommended: SPRT [elo0=0, elo1=25], alpha=beta=0.05, cap at 2000 games; a "non-regression" test for refactors: [elo0=-25, elo1=0]. Always side-swapped pairs on fresh maps; do not reuse tuning seeds (map_base 1000+gen*games) - use map_base >= 900000 for evaluation.
- Tuning-time noise: per-candidate fitness SE ~0.056 with 80 games; population spread of true skill differences is probably < 0.05, so selection is noise-dominated early. That is why big popsize/mean-based update + re-evaluation matters.

### Q6: overfitting to the tuning pool; held-out detection
- Mechanism: parameters co-adapt to the clone's quirks (BC nets are mimicry; a clone is weaker/odder than its source in fog edge cases). Detect as in rec 6. Also check game-level signatures: if win share vs anchors is high but average game length collapses (more early rush kills of a clone that is bad at defence), that is overfitting to a clone weakness.
- Hold-out design: different style AND different generator: nanomena-BC (other human style), ResBot-BC at argmax, expander, a frozen early version, and (if the integration agent builds it) any heuristic ext bot. Also holdout on maps: keep a fixed set of ~60 evaluation seeds never used by tuning.
- Cheap sanity: Elo ladder of checkpoints vs the same held-out set should be monotone (checkpoint 5 > 3 > 1). Non-monotone = noise or overfit.
- Literature: coevolution Hall-of-Fame, AlphaStar "fixed-eval" style benchmarks; quant-eagle uses a "fixed-eval sidecar" for the same reason [V].

### Q8 (new): robustness vs strong neural/RL bots
Documented RL weaknesses:
- arXiv 2507.06825: agent "can get stuck in dead ends formed by mountains and not knowing how to get out" due to lack of look-ahead and a CNN's inability to compute distances; "stuck in some specific mode" - only attacking, defending or castle-taking without balancing (e.g. castle-capture while ignoring an active attack). [V] https://arxiv.org/html/2507.06825v1
- AverageJoe paper: no systematic weakness analysis; says representations are distributed/uninterpretable; one head tracks opponent-general belief; robustness sensitivity left for future work. [V] https://arxiv.org/html/2606.23348v1 . It beat Human.exe and zero v3 20-0 each, 199-70 vs top humans, so a full-size AverageJoe would be much stronger than us; but an entrant must fit 150 ms on one core, which implies smaller nets [I].
- quant-eagle README: deterministic greedy policy "is exploitable in principle", no exploitability bound, no ablations. [V]
Exploit/robustness list [I]:
1. Late game: their training games mostly end before turn ~600 (our replay stats: only ~7% of finals pass turn 800), so deathtouch-phase behaviour (fortress, chase kill from a third tile, simultaneous touch = draw) is likely under-trained. Keep fortress_turn/dt_stage_turn tuned and test against ResBot-BC at TEMP 0, which is itself imitation of a human-like bot.
2. Determinism: greedy argmax RL bots respond identically to identical observations; within a game a feint (stack appears then retreats) can draw its army; only test where cheap.
3. Fog beliefs: RL bots infer from EMA/history planes. Our exact accounting (castle price -> ring around their general) gives information they have to learn implicitly.
4. Mountain dead-ends: when attacking, path the army so the opponent's responding stack must detour; never plan our own routes through pockets (our BFS handles it).
5. Mode fixation: schedule our deep strike when the opponent has just started a castle gather; keep garrison discipline when it is attacking.
6. Zero forfeits: a 10M-param ViT on CPU in 150 ms is borderline; if their evaluator is loaded (shared core) they may time out. Nothing for us to do except never be slow ourselves.
7. Training-side: add an RL-style sparring partner by BC of ResBot (exists) at two temperatures (0 and 0.3-0.5); the temperature mix covers "greedy" and "stochastic" RL deployments.

### CMA-ES under noise (supporting facts)
- Reevaluation / population size adaptation / learning-rate adaptation are the three standard noise-handling families; UH-CMA-ES raises re-evaluation count when ranking changes are detected. [V via search] https://arxiv.org/pdf/2409.16757 , https://arxiv.org/pdf/2304.03473 (CMA-ES with learning-rate adaptation works with default popsize on noisy/multimodal), pycma NoiseHandler https://cma-es.github.io/apidocs-pycma/cma.optimization_tools.NoiseHandler.html
- Practical stance [I]: our noise is large relative to signal early and small late. Use CRN (done), larger fixed games late (2x at hour 13+), and the re-evaluation promotion rule instead of enabling pycma NoiseHandler (it re-evaluates a subset each generation and would change the cost model; it can be tried on the cluster run as the second independent seed: `noise_handler` option, U on exact API).
- SPSA (Fishtest style) is a possible alternative at 40 dims but needs many more games per parameter step; CMA-ES is fine. [V for SPSA description] https://github.com/zamar/spsa , https://www.chessprogramming.org/Automated_Tuning
- Kaggle context: rule-based bots with forward simulation won Halite 2020, Kore 2022 (top three) and Lux S2; Lux S1 was won by deep RL with a frozen-teacher KL stabiliser. [V via search summary] https://www.kaggle.com/writeups/zoli800/winning-kaggles-reinforcement-learning-competitio
